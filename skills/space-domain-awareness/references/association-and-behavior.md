# Association, pattern of life and conjunctions

Part of the `space-domain-awareness` skill. Section numbers (§) match the map in `SKILL.md`.

## 8. Association and identity

- **Gating:** d² = νᵀS⁻¹ν against the 99% χ² value for the measurement dimension (§2) [CV]. A gate is only as good as S: overconfident covariance produces false uncorrelated tracks, inflated covariance produces mis-tags.
- **Ambiguity:** with more than one candidate in the gate, defer (multiple hypothesis tracking or multi-frame assignment) or carry association probabilities. Forced nearest-neighbor assignment in clusters creates cross-tags.
- **Provider tags are claims.** Store a provider's or tasking system's object ID on the observation as an asserted ID and run your own association.
- **Uncorrelated tracks:** tracklet → admissible-region linking across nights → initial orbit → full fit. Promote to a new object only with a consistent multi-night orbit and no catalog object in the gate; at GEO require at least two nights [H].
- **Cross-tags** are endemic in co-located GEO clusters and LEO deployment trains. Signature: two neighbors show simultaneous "maneuvers" of opposite sense, and swapping the tags removes both. Resolve with orbit continuity plus an independent fingerprint (photometry, RF) before any maneuver call. Scale check [H]: co-located satellites keep kilometers apart, so a true cross-tag produces residuals of tens of arcsec or more; an offset of a few arcsec is more likely a blend, a time bias or a real burn.
- **Working recipe:** tangent-plane gates, the two-σ "good / unambiguous" test, GEO-sphere clustering, purity voting, the frame-to-frame tracker and the catalog-fusion policy are in §16.4; JPDA/MHT for clutter, track-to-track fusion and multi-night UCT merging in §16.7.
- **Breakups, launches, deployments:** use temporary IDs and expect identities to settle over days to weeks. Do not build PoL on unsettled identities.
- **Identity model:** many-to-many, time-versioned links between catalog number, international designator, provider IDs and uncorrelated-track IDs, each with a probability and method. Keep history; public catalog identities get corrected retroactively.
- **Catalog numbers.** The 5-digit range (1–69999) ran out on 2026-07-11 and new objects are numbered 100000 and up (CelesTrak). What a TLE feed does now depends on the provider: CelesTrak issues no TLE for those objects; Space-Track's gp and gp_history classes emit Alpha-5 in TLE/3LE output (first character a letter, A = 10 … Z = 33, no I or O: 100000 → A0000, 148493 → E8493, 339999 → Z9999); Space-Track's legacy TLE classes stay 5-digit. Every other format carries an integer up to 999,999,999, which fits in int32. So: ingest OMM, key on the integer, decode Alpha-5 explicitly where TLEs must be read, and keep the ID in your own column, because some SGP4 libraries reject numbers above 339999. Naive parsers fail silently (NaN, 0 or dropped sets). Analyst numbers 80000–89999 are reused for different objects: one more reason a catalog number is an attribute in the identity table, not a key.

## 9. Pattern of life and anomalies

**Baselines**
- *GEO, controlled:* the east-west cycle is a longitude parabola inside the deadband with burns near the edge, from weekly to every couple of months for chemical propulsion and near-daily for electric. North-south burns hold the inclination vector. Momentum dumps are tiny.
- *GEO, inclined or fuel-saving:* no north-south control; inclination grows at the natural rate.
- *GEO relocation:* drift orbit above (westward) or below (eastward) GEO, then a stop burn. ΔV follows §2.
- *GEO end of life:* raise to the disposal altitude, drift west at 3°/day or more, then tumble after passivation. Dead in place: libration about a stable longitude, inclination growth, tumbling light curve.
- *LEO:* orbit raising (staircase or continuous), phasing, drag make-up that scales with solar activity, collision avoidance, controlled deorbit or natural decay. Judge each constellation member against its plane or shell peers; the anomaly is the outlier (failed to raise, decaying, out of slot).
- *MEO:* navigation satellites maneuver rarely; a maneuver is itself notable.

**Orbit classes and GEO regime geography [H unless marked]**
- Drift rate from the osculating period: drift = (1 − P/1436.068 min)·360°/day — strictly degrees per revolution, and biased ≈ −0.027°/day at exact GEO because J2 lifts the osculating SMA ≈1.6 km (§2) [physics]. Call an object *drifting* at |drift| ≥ 0.1°/day (≈5× the station-keeping envelope); fold mod 180 so 12-hour orbits are not "drifting"; the quantity is meaningless below ~10,000 km.
- GEO family: perigee ≥ ~34,800 km, or period within ±6 h of a day, or apogee within ±500 km of GEO (transfer orbits). Sub-classes: *stationary* (i < 5°, not drifting), *inclined* (no north–south control), *walker* (drifting, any inclination), *watcher* (a commensurate drifter — sidereal period ÷ |period offset| an integer below 30 within 0.1% — that repeats its fly-bys of every GEO resident at the same phase), *graveyard*, *transfer*, *eccentric* (e > 0.05, loose against station-keeping e ≈ 1e-4). Elsewhere: sun-synchronous (exists only below ≈5,970 km), Molniya (63.4°/116.6°, where J2 apsidal rotation vanishes), equatorial, polar (within 1° of 90°), HEO (e > 0.3).
- Regime bands for screening and alerting: at-GEO ±100 km (≤1.3°/day); near-GEO ±10,000 km; near-graveyard +250 km; graveyard +300 km. Definitions disagree — +200 km in one classifier, IADC's 235 km + 1000·C_R·A/m, +250/+300 km screening bands — so state which one a product uses, and derive "altitude above GEO" from SMA − 42,164 km, the only definition that maps to drift.
- Slot neighbours: both non-drifting, i < 15° (the maximum uncontrolled GEO inclination), enter within ±0.5° of longitude and ±1° of latitude, exit beyond ±0.625° / ±1.25° (hysteresis; the latitude box is wider because inclination-separated co-located satellites oscillate ±i daily).
- Transitions and what they mean: begin/end drift ↔ relocation start/arrival; enter/exit GEO ↔ joining/leaving the ring; near-graveyard → graveyard ↔ disposal under way → about compliant; exit graveyard ↔ anomalous return; dead-at-GEO ↔ an uncontrolled object still in the ring; a drift-state disagreement between the catalog and the curated status ↔ drift that started between fits. Maneuver-class events bound orbit trust (rule 12); photometric, proximity and conjunction events do not.
- Expectation cross-check, a cheap anomaly detector: drifting and graveyard objects should tumble and station-kept GEO objects should not; a mismatch is an anomaly, with two known exceptions — relocating active objects (drifting, controlled) and dead objects librating about a stable longitude (peak drift ≈0.44°/day × sin of the libration amplitude, period ≈2.2 years).
- Severity = contradiction × consequence (rule 13): dead-but-controlled or dead-but-maneuvering above active-tumbling or degraded, above active-drifting. One workable daily ranking sums drifting 1, tumbling 2, degraded 3, proximity or conjunction 3, slot neighbour 0.1 and inactive-in-GEO 1/age in years — recently dead objects dominate, as they should.

**Photometric pattern of life at GEO [H unless marked]**
- Physics: GEO illumination repeats every *solar* day. A healthy three-axis satellite (bus to Earth, arrays to Sun) holds a fixed configuration relative to the Sun–Earth line, so its brightness is a function of phase angle alone — a V about local midnight with slopes ≈ ±0.5 mag/h (≈1 mag per 30° for flat sun-facing panels; a diffuse sphere gives ≲0.1 mag/h). Dead objects follow their rotation, not local time — except spin-stabilized drums and compact bodies, which stay phase-locked without control. Visible array glints cluster at eclipse entry and exit near the equinoxes (§5).
- Fold: seven nights from *one* sensor on local solar time (or signed solar-equatorial phase); cut ±15 min around the *object's* local midnight and never fit a window across it — the equation of time (−14 to +16 min) and topocentric parallax (≤35 min) move the true minimum-phase time by as much as the eclipse half-width; 1-h sliding linear fits; per-side medians of slope and scatter. Features: a slope reversal (a sidereal-style slew ≈1 mag/h), two-sided scatter (tumbling), one-sided scatter (dynamic slewing); quiet scatter ≈0.03 mag, active ≈0.08–0.1. Folding on the sidereal day is right for sensor systematics and wrong for illumination features.
- Output three classes — controlled, tumbling, don't know — as hypotheses for a curator ("tagged X, behaviour indicates Y"), never a verdict: photometry can support "tumbling"; it cannot prove "controlled". A real-time screen: detrend, "controlled" if peak-to-peak < ~10% of the mean, otherwise scatter against noise, with ≥5 detections.
- Period: Lomb–Scargle, phase-dispersion minimization or a transform modulus with a false-alarm probability; check P/2 and 2P (the photometric period is often half the spin period); σ_P ≈ T/(k² − ¼) for a peak in bin k over span T; the GEO period is synodic (shift ≈ P²/48 h).
- Before "a dead object is showing signs of life", rule out mis-association with a co-located active neighbour (§16.4) and run the expectation cross-check above. Known attitude schedules — equinox yaw flips, yaw-steering turns, array feathering (§5) — are baseline, not anomaly.

**Features worth materializing per object:** SMA and drift rate; longitude and deadband occupancy; inclination and eccentricity vectors; maneuver events (epoch, ΔV, radial/transverse/normal direction, confidence, evidence); inter-maneuver intervals; photometric state (stable or tumbling, period, phase-curve parameters, glint offset); RF activity; proximity events (range, relative velocity, geometry, duration); and context flags (coverage gap, storm, season, data quality).

**Methods**
- Change-point detection (PELT, Bayesian online, CUSUM) on element time series; mode labeling in the style of MIT ARCLab's SPLID benchmark (east-west and north-south nodes for initiate station-keeping, initiate drift and adjust drift; modes of not station-keeping, or station-keeping with chemical, electric or hybrid propulsion; scored with F2 at ±12 h tolerance).
- Sequence models (hidden Markov, transformers) and unsupervised detectors (isolation forest, autoencoder) on engineered, physics-aware features, not raw elements.
- An anomaly is a deviation from the object's own baseline and from its peers, normalized by uncertainty and conditioned on context.
- Evaluation: labels are scarce, so use operator-published maneuvers and ephemerides as truth, synthetic injection and historical replays. Split by time, build features point-in-time (§12), and pick the metric from the cost: F2 when misses cost more, precision at a fixed alert budget for an analyst queue.

**Public case signatures**
- Docking or servicing: photometric fingerprint change (MEV-2 and Intelsat 10-02).
- Tug: a large, unannounced orbit change of a dead object. SJ-21 docked with a defunct BeiDou satellite during a daytime gap in ground optical coverage and towed it about 3,000 km above GEO (January 2022).
- Serial inspector: repeated relocations ending tens of km from other operators' satellites (Luch/Olymp-K and Luch/Olymp 2).
- Sub-satellite release and matched-plane shadowing of another satellite, then release of a further object (Cosmos 2542/2543, 2019–2020).
- Paired inspectors bracketing a target (commercial tracking analysis reported this for GSSAP around Shijian-29 A/B, March 2026).

**Deception-aware detection [H].** Expect maneuvers timed to coverage gaps, object swaps or decoys during a custody gap (COMSPOC's analysis of TJS-3 and its kick motor, May 2019), maneuvers masked by storms, and behavior that mimics benign servicing. After any custody gap with a position discontinuity, re-verify identity before extending PoL.

## 10. Conjunctions

- 2D probability of collision (Foster, Chan, Alfano, Patera) assumes a short, high-relative-velocity encounter with Gaussian position error. Slow or long encounters (GEO neighbors, formations, proximity operations) need 3D or Monte Carlo methods [CV].
- Pc is a function of covariance realism. An oversized covariance dilutes Pc; a low Pc in the dilution region is not evidence of safety [C]. Never compute Pc from GP/TLE states.
- **NASA [PS]** (NPR 8079.1; Conjunction Assessment Best Practices Handbook Rev 2, August 2026): Pc above 1E-7 merits attention. The mitigation threshold is Pc above 1E-4 at the mitigation commitment point, or a miss distance below the combined hard-body radius. A mitigation should cut Pc by at least 1.5 orders of magnitude (to about 3E-6).
- **19th Space Defense Squadron [PS]** (Spaceflight Safety Handbook v1.7, April 2023). Conjunction messages are published for near-Earth events (period under 225 min) with closest approach within 3 days, miss ≤1 km and Pc ≥1E-7, and for deep-space events within 10 days and ≤5 km. Emergency criteria: near Earth within 3 days, miss ≤1 km and Pc ≥1E-4; deep space within 3 days and ≤5 km. Catalog screening volumes (radial × in-track × cross-track, km): 0.4 × 44 × 51 for perigee ≤500 km, stepping down to 0.4 × 2 × 2 for 1,200–2,000 km; 10 × 10 × 10 in deep space. Operator-ephemeris screening uses 2 × 25 × 25 near Earth and 20 × 20 × 20 in deep space. Read the handbook tables before implementing.
- Thresholds are the operator's risk policy, not a constant; some operators act orders of magnitude below 1E-4. Make them per-customer configuration.
- Store the Pc method, hard-body radius (Pc scales roughly with its square) and covariance age with every value. A missing Pc means not computed, not safe: fall back to miss distance.
- Check a conjunction message before acting on it: age of the last observation, number of tracks, covariance plausibility, and whether the other object is maneuverable or has an operator ephemeris.

**Screening validity, context and geometry [H unless marked]**
- Validity is bounded by maneuvers (rule 12): never screen across a known burn of either object; publish a past encounter between active objects only with observations *after* TCA; a new maneuver orbit may not rewrite a past event; replace a published result only when σ improves (≈1 m of hysteresis against churn); de-duplicate repeats within ±P/8; keep the screening cadence under P/4 so a TCA within a quarter period of processing is always caught.
- Context multipliers rather than new thresholds, defaulting to "unknown ≠ same operator": same-operator active pairs ÷2, dead–dead ÷5, the k-σ margin halved per dead object, frequent relocators and protected assets ×. Treat *proximity* (~25 km, non-cooperating operators only) as an intent signal and *conjunction* (~10 km, with miss + kσ ≲ 20 km) as a physical hazard regardless of trust; reporting-only neighbourhood screens run to ~100 km at under 10 m/s.
- Sampling guarantee: the separation rate is bounded by v₁ + v₂, so a sample within dt/2 of TCA misses the true minimum by at most V·dt/2 — screen at a radius D above the standoff R with dt ≤ 2(D − R)/V, or skip ahead while s − V·Δt > R (head-on closing at GEO is ≈6.6 km/s: a 10 km screen needs ≤ ~1.5 s or an adaptive step). Pad apsides for decay, drop element sets older than ~30 days, and **test every prefilter against brute force**: a longitude wrap, a cluster merge, a grid bound and an apsidal guard have each been subtly unsafe. Closest-approach search methods and Pc beyond 2D are in §16.7.
- Miss σ: project P₁ + P₂ (minus cross-correlation from shared sensors) onto the miss direction in the encounter plane; the trace overstates by √3 to 10–30× [physics]. Pc ∝ hard-body radius² and is dilution-prone; a square hard body is ≈27% conservative; fixed 5 + 1 m bodies understate large GEO comsats; scale a covariance by congruence (S·P·Sᵀ), never by its diagonal blocks alone.
- The RPO pattern as pair flags: altitude, drift and inclination match; small period difference; low two-impulse (Lambert) ΔV; proximity under 100 km at under 10 m/s; deployment = new object + close + cheap. A plane change costs 54 m/s per degree (§2), so an inclination match produced by a burn is strong evidence of intent. Geometry in the target's Hill frame: a radial offset Δr drifts along-track at −1.5·n·Δr ≈ 9.4 km/day per km at GEO; the cross-track amplitude ≈ a·i_rel; from a ground sensor 1 km at GEO is ≈5″, about one PSF, so a closer pair merges and cross-tags (§8); object-to-object phase angles tell who sees whom — a low chaser→target phase (Sun behind the chaser, target fully lit) is inspection, or stalking, geometry [CV].
- Anti-patterns: distance-only "Pc" curves (policy equivalences such as 200 km ↔ 1e-6, not probabilities — they saturate, grow with σ and scale linearly with size); Pc = 0 or 1e-99 for a missing covariance (hides "not computable"; use miss distance and say so); a secondary covariance written in the primary's RTN (CCSDS puts each in its own); thresholds set above the screening radius (they never fire).
- Launch collision avoidance: per-class standoff and Pc thresholds, crewed objects always by standoff; element sets under 30 days old; apsis prefilter padded ~0.1%; a time step of 0.1–1% of the secondary's period with skip-ahead; closures refined by a minimum-distance search; re-time an ascent by keeping its Earth-fixed state (exact only under axisymmetric forces).
- Reporting a close approach (§13.5): TCA in UTC to the second; miss distance ± σ with the σ type; relative speed; solar phase at TCA and which object has the lighting advantage; both objects' recent burns and station-keeping habit; the "no further maneuvers" assumption; the source and age of the secondary's state; for a negative screen, its radius. Issue a day ahead and again on the day; re-run after any burn by either object and show the previous value; co-located pairs approach about twice a day. Add the approached object's neighbours to the watch *before* the encounter so photometric baselines exist.
